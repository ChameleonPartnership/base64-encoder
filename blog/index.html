Base64 Encoder Blog

Base64 is one of those formats every developer meets but few stop to study. It shows up in API payloads, email attachments, image tags, and authentication headers, yet the reasons behind its design are often skipped. This blog explains how base64 actually works, why it exists, and when using it helps or hurts your application.

Each article covers a different angle: the history and theory of the encoding, its everyday role in web development, and the specific trade-offs of embedding images as data URLs. If you have ever wondered whether to encode binary data inline or serve it as a file, these guides give you a clear, practical answer.

One number is worth remembering before you start: base64 adds roughly 33 percent to the size of the data it encodes. Three binary bytes become four ASCII characters, which is the price you pay for safely transporting binary data through text-only channels. That overhead is trivial for small payloads and significant for large files, which is why the guides here spend real time on the trade-off between inline encoding and plain file delivery.
